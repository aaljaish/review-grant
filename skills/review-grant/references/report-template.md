# Required report format

Use Markdown unless the user requests another format. Adapt length to the supplied application and requested scope. Retain the three table columns below. The template is a structure, not permission to invent content or fill a numerical quota.

## Review scope

List the materials and versions actually reviewed, inaccessible or missing materials, requested focus, and sponsor criteria if provided. State whether literature, guidelines, registries, numerical assumptions, or other external evidence were independently checked. Include dates and limits for any searches.

## Overall reviewability

Give a brief assessment explaining what a thoughtful non-specialist reviewer can understand and where essential inference is required. Recognize relevant documented strengths. Avoid an unsupported score or funding prediction.

Identify the **three changes most helpful to reviewability**. These concern how easily a reviewer can follow and assess the argument. They may overlap with the highest-priority substantive revisions later. If fewer than three are supported, say so.

## Four-domain assessment

Briefly address importance, coherence, feasibility, and usefulness. Mark a domain not assessed when the supplied scope does not support judgment. Do not repeat every concern in full here and again in the table.

## Reviewer concern table

| Potential reviewer concern | Why it matters | Suggested revision |

| **Major or Minor; domain.** Evidence location and a specific concern. Label unclear, not found in supplied material, or not assessed when applicable. | <ul><li>Explain the consequence for judging the proposal.</li><li>Separate documented facts from interpretation.</li></ul> | <ul><li>Name a concrete revision or evidence request.</li><li>Identify any needed team decision or specialist input.</li></ul> |

Use one concern per row. Use concise bullets within the second and third columns. HTML lists inside table cells are acceptable for Markdown renderers that support them; use multiline cells with real bullets for a Word or other document table. If the renderer cannot display lists in cells, use numbered concern blocks retaining the same three labels and explain the formatting adaptation.

Do not add extra severity or evidence columns. Put severity, domain, and location in the first column. Include only supported concerns. Combine duplicates across domains where appropriate.

## Five highest-priority changes before submission

Provide up to five distinct actions in priority order. For each, state the requested change and its principal reason. An owner or dependency can be useful, but assign responsibility to a role only when justified and label suggestions as suggestions. If fewer than five concerns are supported, report fewer rather than inventing problems. These priorities may include substantive design or operational concerns, beyond the three reviewability improvements above.

## Hypothetical committee discussion

Write one short paragraph illustrating what a reviewer might say if the unresolved concerns remain. Label it hypothetical. Ground it in this review, acknowledge relevant strengths, and do not imply that a real committee met or that a funding decision is known.

## Questions and human review

List material unresolved questions or evidence needs. Close with a brief reminder that the study team should adjudicate the AI output with methodological, statistical, operational, and patient or community reviewers where relevant. Do not imply that AI review replaces scientific, ethics, regulatory, or sponsor review.
