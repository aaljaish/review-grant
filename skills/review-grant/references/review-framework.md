# Review framework

Use this framework to find consequential, evidenced concerns. Questions guide judgment; they are not a checklist that every application must answer in identical prose. Apply design-specific questions only when relevant.

## Overall reviewability

Can a non-specialist reviewer follow the trial question, rationale, design, assumptions, and intended decision after one careful read? Where must the reviewer infer missing information or reconstruct logic across disconnected sections? Identify the three changes most likely to improve reviewability, linking them to evidence in the application.

Distinguish a difficult explanation from an unresolved scientific choice. If wording makes it impossible to tell which is present, explain what clarification would allow a reviewer to judge it.

## 1. Importance: Is the trial worth doing?

- Identify the unresolved question, affected population, current practice, and uncertainty that justifies the investment.
- Assess whether randomization is justified and uncertainty or equipoise is demonstrated through evidence rather than asserted.
- Ask why an RCT is needed rather than implementation, quality improvement, access reform, de-implementation, knowledge translation, or another study design. Do not assume these alternatives are always preferable or mutually exclusive.
- Assess the treatment of prior studies, guidelines, and ongoing trials, including registry information when available. Distinguish an incomplete literature account from an independently verified evidence gap.
- Look for a clear explanation of what this trial would add and why the unanswered question matters to patients, communities, clinicians, health systems, or policy makers as appropriate.

Useful revision: identify the particular uncertainty, summarize evidence bearing on it, and explain why the proposed comparison can resolve it. Request supporting sources when they are absent.

## 2. Coherence: Do the trial elements align?

Trace the connection among trial intent, design, comparator, unit of randomization, eligibility, intervention, outcome timing, follow-up, data sources, estimand, sample size, and analysis plan.

- **Intent and design:** Are pragmatic, explanatory, pilot, feasibility, or definitive labels supported by actual design choices? Are superiority, noninferiority, or equivalence aims reflected in outcomes and analysis where relevant?
- **Comparison:** Are the intervention and comparator specified enough to understand what participants receive, how delivery varies, and how co-interventions may affect interpretation?
- **Estimand:** Can a reviewer identify the treatment conditions, population, outcome variable, handling of intercurrent events, and population-level summary of effect? Are these consistent with the stated question and primary analysis? Do not force technical terminology when the relevant choices are already explicit in ordinary language.
- **Primary outcome:** Is it decision-relevant, valid for the intended purpose, clearly defined, and measured at a justified time? Are collection methods and data sources credible for the population and setting?
- **Sample size or precision:** Are the target effect or precision, assumptions, data sources, uncertainty, and sensitivity to alternative assumptions visible? Consider event rates or variability, attrition, and design features such as clustering where relevant. For pilots, judge the rationale against feasibility objectives rather than automatically requiring definitive efficacy power.
- **Analysis:** Is the primary analysis auditable rather than generic? Are the analysis population, effect measure, adjustment, design features, missing data, adherence, crossovers, contamination, clustering, and co-interventions addressed where relevant?
- **Consistency:** Are sample counts, follow-up periods, outcome definitions, and trial milestones consistent across documents? Does the analysis estimate an effect that answers the intended question?

Useful revision: reconcile the specific conflicting choices, show assumptions and their sources, or provide a compact question-to-analysis mapping. Do not select a new effect size or analytic strategy without the team's justification.

## 3. Feasibility: Can the trial be delivered?

- Are recruitment, retention, site activation, workflow, intervention delivery, data access, and timeline assumptions supported by relevant evidence?
- Do the operational plans support the assumptions used for sample size, missingness, follow-up, and analysis?
- Are recruitment denominators, eligibility, consent, site throughput, and ramp-up periods distinguished? When doing arithmetic, account for whether sites are active concurrently and avoid double-counting attrition or consent losses.
- Are intervention delivery, fidelity, staffing, training, data extraction, linkage, measurement, and resources credible for the proposed setting?
- Are the budget and timeline consistent with the promised activities, if those materials were supplied?
- Are team roles, governance, decision-making, escalation pathways, and accountability clear?
- Are ethics, consent, privacy, participant burden, safety monitoring, and equity operationalized in relation to the actual design and setting? Identify reviewer questions without asserting compliance with laws or sponsor rules that have not been checked.
- Are major delivery dependencies and practical contingencies visible? Ask for evidence or a defensible plan, rather than inventing assurances or agreements.

Useful revision: connect the target to a documented denominator, observed rate, implementation commitment, or resource plan, and explain what happens if the assumption is not met.

## 4. Usefulness: Will the answer matter?

- Identify who will use the result and the clinical, health system, policy, implementation, or future research decision it will inform.
- Does the proposal explain how beneficial, null, and inconclusive results would each be useful? Avoid treating a non-significant result as proof of no effect; assess precision and the decisions the evidence could support.
- Is the primary comparison relevant to those decisions, including the setting and timing of use?
- Does knowledge translation identify actions, decision makers, engagement, and pathways beyond publications and conference presentations?
- For pilot or feasibility trials, are feasibility objectives, progression criteria, and the next-step decision explicit? Are criteria interpretable and linked to what will proceed, change, or stop?
- Are patient or community contributions described where relevant to the question, delivery, interpretation, or use of findings?

Useful revision: specify the decision, the responsible audience, the evidence needed, and how different plausible results would influence the next action.

## Common weaknesses to flag when supported

| Pattern | What the reviewer needs to judge |
| --- | --- |
| Important topic, unclear unresolved question | The exact uncertainty and why answering it changes a decision |
| Equipoise asserted | The evidence supporting remaining uncertainty |
| Poorly defined or justified comparator | The actual contrast and its relevance to practice |
| Trial label mismatched to choices | How the design serves the stated intent |
| Estimand unclear or inconsistent | The effect being estimated and its link to analysis |
| Convenient primary outcome | Why the measure is valid and decision-relevant |
| Outcome timing unsupported | Why the follow-up captures the intended effect |
| Unsupported sample size assumptions | Assumption sources and consequences of plausible alternatives |
| Missing treatment of missingness or intercurrent events | How the proposed analysis addresses relevant events and missing observations |
| Overlooked adherence, crossovers, contamination, clustering, or co-interventions | Whether design and analysis support the intended interpretation |
| Feasibility asserted | Evidence connecting targets to deliverable activity |
| Unclear roles or governance | Who decides, acts, escalates, and is accountable |
| Generic ethics or equity language | Design-specific actions, responsibilities, and monitoring |
| Translation limited to dissemination | The pathway from evidence to a named decision |
| Pilot without progression logic | The feasibility result that determines the next step |

These patterns are prompts for inspection, not findings to insert automatically.
