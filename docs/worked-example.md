# Fictional worked example

All trial details below are invented solely to demonstrate the review format. They describe no real application, patient, institution, study, or funding decision. They must not be reused as feasibility evidence.

## Supplied example excerpt

**Document:** `fictional-application.txt`, version 1.

**Section A, question:** "We will test whether a discharge follow-up call reduces unplanned readmission compared with usual discharge care. Readmissions create a substantial burden. A randomized trial is necessary."

**Section B, design:** "This pragmatic, individually randomized definitive trial will enroll 600 adults across four hospitals. The primary outcome is unplanned readmission within 30 days. The sample size assumes a 20% relative reduction in readmission, 80% power, and a two-sided 5% significance level."

**Section C, delivery:** "Each hospital will recruit 10 participants per month for 12 months. All sites will recruit throughout this period. Follow-up will use hospital administrative data."

**Section D, analysis:** "We will use intention-to-treat analysis with appropriate statistical methods."

**Section E, impact:** "We will publish the findings and present them at conferences."

No other application sections, sponsor criteria, or supporting evidence were supplied for this example.

## Example review

### Review scope

Reviewed Sections A to E of `fictional-application.txt`, version 1. No protocol, budget, outcome ascertainment specification, prior-evidence summary, or operational support was supplied. Literature, guidelines, and trial registries were not independently checked. The recruitment calculation below checks internal consistency only.

### Overall reviewability

The basic comparison and primary outcome are understandable. A reviewer cannot yet judge why randomization is needed, whether the proposed sample size is justified, or how the analysis will estimate the intended effect. The stated recruitment plan also falls short of the enrollment target. These concerns involve information not found or unclear in the excerpt and do not prove that the team lacks supporting work elsewhere.

The three changes most helpful to reviewability are to reconcile the recruitment target with the operational plan, show the question-to-analysis logic with auditable assumptions, and explain the unresolved evidence gap alongside the decision the trial will inform.

### Four-domain assessment

- **Importance:** The topic's burden is asserted, but the unresolved question and justification for randomization need an evidence-based account.
- **Coherence:** The primary outcome and timing are named. The treatment contrast, effect definition, sample size assumptions, and analysis are not sufficiently specified to assess alignment.
- **Feasibility:** The supplied recruitment arithmetic is inconsistent with the target. Other delivery assumptions cannot be judged from this excerpt.
- **Usefulness:** Publications and presentations identify dissemination routes, but no decision maker or decision pathway is named.

### Five highest-priority changes before submission

1. Reconcile the 600-person target with the 480-person recruitment plan and provide supporting operational evidence.
2. Supply an auditable sample size rationale and primary analysis aligned with the intended treatment effect.
3. Demonstrate the unresolved evidence gap and justification for randomization.
4. Define the intervention contrast and confirm that primary outcome ascertainment supports it.
5. Connect possible results to a named decision and a practical pathway to use the findings.

### Hypothetical committee discussion

"The comparison is easy to understand and readmission is a potentially important outcome, but the excerpt leaves key assumptions difficult to judge. The recruitment plan does not reach the target, the statistical rationale needs more detail, and the proposal does not yet show what decision the findings would change. These issues would need clarification before we could assess the investment confidently."

### Questions and human review

Are supporting evidence, data-access arrangements, and analysis specifications documented elsewhere? What is the decision-relevant effect, and what prior evidence supports the baseline assumptions? The study team should adjudicate this illustrative review with methodological, statistical, operational, and patient or community reviewers as relevant.
